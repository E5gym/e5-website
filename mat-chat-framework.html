The Science

The Mat Chat Framework

Mat chats are short conversations that connect character to training. They should not become lectures. A good mat chat is clear, real, and tied to what is happening in class.

A mat chat works best when the coach uses something real from class, asks one useful question, and sends students back into training with a challenge.

The 4-Step Formula

Keep it short enough to use.

1

Name the idea

Example: "Today we're talking about courage."

2

Connect it to training

Example: "Courage shows up when you try something even though you feel nervous."

3

Ask one question

Example: "Where might you need courage today?"

4

Give a challenge

Example: "When something feels hard today, take one breath and try again."

The Coach's Compass

Before speaking, take 60 seconds to answer.

What actually happened in class today that connects to this word?

What do I want students to notice?

What question will make them think?

What simple challenge can they carry back into training?

Common Mistakes

What weakens a mat chat.

Talking too long

If students are waiting too long, the point gets weaker.

Teaching it like school

Do not explain the word like a vocabulary lesson.

Too many questions

One useful question is stronger than five scattered ones.

Too abstract

Connect the word to a real moment students can see.

Disconnected examples

Use examples from class, not random stories.

No challenge

End by giving students one simple way to practice the idea.

The Rule

Keep it under 3 minutes. One idea. One question. One challenge. Then get back to training.
