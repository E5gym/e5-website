Class Management

Lead the Room.

Class management is not about controlling kids. It is about creating enough structure, energy, and clarity for students to train safely and stay engaged.

A good coach manages the room before chaos becomes the room.

Core Principle

The room follows the coach's state.

If the coach is unclear, the room becomes unclear. If the coach is frantic, the room becomes frantic. If the coach is calm, direct, and present, the room has a better chance of becoming calm, direct, and present.

Earth — Structure

Clear boundaries, clean stations, simple rules, and predictable class flow.

Fire — Presence

Voice, energy, confidence, and the ability to bring the room back.

Air — Awareness

Seeing the room early enough to adjust before problems grow.

Quick Response

When the class starts drifting, do this first:

  1. Stop and reset the room.
  2. Lower the complexity.
  3. Give one clear instruction.
  4. Restart with energy.
  5. Praise the first students who respond correctly.

What to Say

Use clear words under pressure.

To reset the room

"Freeze. Eyes here."

"Bring it in. We are resetting."

"This is not working yet. Let's fix the system."

To simplify

"One job right now."

"Make the first piece clean."

"Slow it down and show control."

To redirect

"Try that again with focus."

"That choice is not helping the group."

"You can join correctly or take a reset."

To encourage

"That was closer."

"You stayed with it."

"Good correction. Do it again."

To set a boundary

"That is unsafe."

"That is not an option."

"You can rejoin when you are ready to follow the rule."

Do not wait until the class is out of control.

Class management is early action: early structure, early redirection, early simplification, early praise, and early boundaries.

The earlier you act, the less force you need.
