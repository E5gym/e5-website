Skill with purpose

More than techniques.

Elemental Martial Arts (EMA) is a modern practical program bringing together striking, grappling, takedowns, ground skills, and practical self-defense within a progressive martial arts system.

Rooted in Japanese martial traditions and informed by modern combatives, EMA develops capable, confident students who understand that physical skill carries responsibility.

Our goal isn't simply to teach people how to fight. It's to develop the ability, awareness, and judgment to act appropriately when it matters.

Skill With responsibility

One integrated program

Rooted in tradition. Built for practical training.

Elemental Martial Arts draws from several martial arts disciplines, combining traditional knowledge with modern approaches to self-defense. These are curriculum influences and training areas—not separate style-specific classes.

Budo Taijutsu (Ninjutsu)

An important foundation in Japanese martial movement, evasion, striking, grappling, and the relationship between balance and technique.

Jujutsu, Judo & Aikijutsu

Principles of leverage, throws, takedowns, joint control, and standing grappling.

Karate & Kickboxing

Striking fundamentals, footwork, combinations, timing, and defensive responses.

Ground Fighting & Wrestling

Positional control, escapes, transitions, and grappling under pressure.

Krav Maga & Modern Combatives

Practical self-defense, awareness, decisive responses, and opportunities to escape dangerous situations.

Rather than separating these disciplines into unrelated classes, EMA integrates their techniques and principles into one progressive training system. Students develop striking, standing grappling, ground skills, and practical self-defense over time; these are progressive areas of study, not guarantees of proficiency.

One program · Two pathways

Training for each stage

EMA is one integrated program with youth and teen/adult pathways. Shared principles guide training while instruction and challenges suit the people in the room.

Youth pathway

Confidence built through discipline.

Structured, age-appropriate training challenges students physically and mentally. They develop coordination, balance, defensive movement, and foundational martial arts skills while practicing respect, patience, cooperation, and self-control.

As students mature, training becomes progressively more technical, encouraging independence, perseverance, and thoughtful decision-making.

  • Coordination and balance
  • Defensive movement
  • Respect and cooperation
  • Perseverance and self-control

Teen & adult pathway

Practical skills. Meaningful challenges.

Teen and adult training explores practical self-defense through striking, grappling, takedowns, escapes, and progressively more complex technical challenges.

Students learn to manage distance, maintain balance, recognize opportunities, and respond with greater composure under pressure. Consistent practice develops physical capability alongside awareness, restraint, and confidence.

  • Striking and grappling
  • Takedowns and escapes
  • Composure and awareness
  • Restraint and judgment

Understand movement

Principles before complexity.

Learn how movement works, not just what to do.

Good martial arts training develops understanding as well as technique. Throughout EMA, students explore structure, balance, distance, timing, leverage, positioning, control, and adaptation.

Structure & balance

Build stable, adaptable movement and understand how balance shapes a response.

Distance & timing

Notice space and opportunity as situations and movement change.

Leverage & positioning

Explore how placement and efficient movement influence control.

Control

Practice deliberate movement with care for training partners and others.

Adaptation

Connect principles to changing circumstances instead of relying on a single response.

These principles connect individual techniques into a broader understanding of movement and self-defense. They also encourage patience, awareness, and deliberate action.

A cumulative learning process

Earned through practice. Demonstrated through skill.

EMA uses a structured progression system that encourages students to build skills over time through progressive technical expectations, skill demonstrations, and cumulative evaluations.

01 · Practice

Build understanding

Students develop physical capability and technical knowledge over time.

02 · Planned model

Four stripes per belt

The intended four-stripe belt model would recognize development along the way; the model is planned and subject to final confirmation.

03 · Demonstrate

Demonstrate skill

Skill demonstrations offer opportunities to show developing understanding.

04 · Evaluate

Cumulative evaluations

Evaluations consider skills and understanding built across a student's training.

Progress reflects more than memorizing techniques. It includes growing understanding, physical capability, consistency, and responsibility. No belt colors, timelines, or rank requirements are specified here.

Fire

One of E5's five elements

Fire: learning to act.

Within the Elemental 5 philosophy, Fire represents courage, effort, discipline, and the willingness to act. Martial arts gives those qualities a physical expression.

Students learn to meet challenges, manage discomfort, exercise restraint, and recognize that strength is most meaningful when guided by good judgment.

We train to become capable. We practice to become disciplined. We develop character through how we choose to act.
